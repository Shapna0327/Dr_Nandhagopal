What Does a Pulmonologist Do?

A pulmonologist is a doctor who specialises in diagnosing and treating diseases affecting respiratory system (Lungs and Airways).

Lungs Airways Breathing Sleep-Related Breathing
"If you have persistent cough, breathlessness, wheezing, sleep-related breathing concerns or other respiratory symptoms, a pulmonology consultation may help identify the underlying cause."

Areas of Respiratory Care

Comprehensive evaluation and management of various respiratory, pulmonary, and sleep-related conditions.

Asthma & COPD

Management of obstructive airway diseases, helping patients understand their triggers and optimize their breathing function.

Sleep-Related Breathing

Evaluation of sleep disorders affecting respiratory function, aiming to improve sleep quality and overall respiratory health.

Respiratory Infections

Diagnosis and care for various pulmonary conditions, respiratory tract infections, and post-infection respiratory management.

Interventional Pulmonology

Experience in advanced diagnostic procedures, including fibre-optic bronchoscopy and allied pulmonary assessments.

Respiratory Critical Care

Expertise in ventilatory management and care for patients requiring intensive respiratory support.

Pulmonary Function Assessment

Comprehensive testing including Spirometry, FENO, and DLCO to accurately assess lung capacity and airway function.

When Should You Consider a Consultation?

Persistent or concerning symptoms deserve professional medical evaluation. Consider seeking a pulmonology assessment if you experience:

Persistent cough
Unexplained breathlessness
Wheezing or chest tightness
Recurrent respiratory issues
Sleep-related breathing concerns
Smoking-related lung concerns

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a pulmonologist treat?
A pulmonologist specializes in diagnosing and managing conditions affecting the respiratory system. This includes the lungs and airways. Common conditions evaluated include asthma, Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), respiratory infections, and sleep-related breathing disorders.
What is the difference between a pulmonologist and a general physician?
While a general physician provides primary care for a wide range of health issues, a pulmonologist has specific advanced training (such as an MD in Tuberculosis and Respiratory Diseases) focused entirely on the respiratory system. They handle more complex, chronic, or specific lung-related conditions.
Do I need a referral to consult Dr. Nandagopal?
You do not strictly need a referral to book a consultation, though many patients are referred by their general practitioners if specialized respiratory assessment is required. You can contact the clinic directly to schedule an appointment.
What should I bring to my first appointment?
Please bring any previous medical records, x-rays, CT scans, pulmonary function test results, and a list of your current medications. This helps in providing a comprehensive evaluation of your respiratory health.
